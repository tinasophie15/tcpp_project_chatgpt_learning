Impact of ChatGPT on the Academic Writing Quality of Senior High School Students

This page summarizes the study by Maghamil and Sieras (2024), which investigated whether integrating ChatGPT impacts the writing quality of Grade 12 students using a pretest-posttest experimental design.

The Study at a Glance

n = 54

Grade 12 TVL students participated in the study.

27 + 27

control group and ChatGPT-assisted group.

Pre-Post

experimental design was used to measure writing quality.

3 Raters

independent examiners analyzed the students' outputs.

Study Overview

Design

Comparison between two Groups

One group wrote their academic assignments traditionally, while the other group integrated ChatGPT in learning writing skills.

Task

Academic writing development

Students underwent pretests and posttests to examine changes in proficiency across content, grammar, mechanics, and organization.

Participants

Senior High School students

The participants were Grade 12 TVL tracks facing practical challenges in traditional writing skill development.

Assessment

Writing quality and efficiency

The researchers measured quantitative data using statistical tools: frequency, percentage, mean, and t-tests.

Main findings

Finding 1

Initial writing quality was low

The average writing quality during the pretest was poor and fair for the control group and fair for the experimental group.

Finding 2

ChatGPT improved writing quality

The experimental group's writing quality significantly improved to a satisfactory level during the posttest.

Finding 3

Control group remained stagnant

The control group, which used traditional writing methods without AI assistance, remained at a fair level during the posttest.

Finding 4

Core metrics enhanced

Based on significant differences, the study concludes that ChatGPT impacts writing quality in terms of content, grammar, mechanics, and organization.

Why is this study relevant?

Language models such as ChatGPT can impact the writing quality of Grade 12 TVL students in terms of content, grammar, mechanics, and organization.

This study is relevant because it provides empirical evidence supporting the benefits of AI integration. While many fear AI replaces skills, this article shows it can be a beneficial and efficient tool for teaching writing skills when integrated properly into instructional materials.

Maghamil & Sieras (2024) show that large language models improve the writing quality of Grade 12 students, showing that AI can be a powerful educational equalizer for developing writing proficiency.
